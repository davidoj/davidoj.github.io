Paper

Journal of Causal Inference, 2025 · with Cheng Soon Ong & Robert C. Williamson

When are causal interventions justified?

Causal models treat actions as clean “interventions” on a system — but what are interventions? Are they physical actions stemming from a principal’s decisions, an idealisation of randomised experiments, or something else? Starting from decision theory, we show how symmetry and parametric non-degeneracy assumptions can justify — or fail to justify — intervention-based reasoning.

Conceptually original foundations work with the mathematics to back it up. It matters more, not less, as we start asking causal questions about AI systems themselves.

Kaibernetic — an AI consultant

AI tools open up your frontier of possibilities — suddenly there’s far more you could be doing. It’s also deceptive: a demo built with AI assistants is often a small fraction of the real work. Kaibernetic is my attempt to nurture that inspiration: project-management infrastructure plus AI skills to help you turn it into something amazing, building self-sustaining feedback loops that carry you all the way through. The nearest human job to what it does is a consultant.
